# Project Brief — Artificial Life / Evolution Simulator

**Source:** Greg, Aug 18 2026. This document records *his* stated asks, goals, constraints, and open
questions only. It contains no proposals, ideas, or interpretations from Claude except where a
clarified restatement is explicitly labeled as such.

---

## 0. Framing

- This has been attempted many times before and has failed each time. He wants to try again **afresh** —
  valiantly, not incrementally.
- He expects a **novel, valuable breakthrough**, not a variation on prior work.
- Reference minds for the work: a *pragmatic fusion of Dave Ackley and Terence Tao*.
- Nothing is to be built yet. The ask at this stage is: think, research, and propose a plan.

---

## 1. The Core Requirement

> Extremely simple, legible rules leading to emergent complexity via natural selection and evolution,
> and ideally eventually **co-evolution** from species descended from the same simpler common ancestor.

---

## 2. The Three Goals (equally most important)

### Goal 1 — Legibility

- Easy to follow moment to moment.
- Few or no hidden states.
- Local interactions.
- Simplicity and elegance.

### Goal 2 — Co-evolution, and environment as a record of history

Two parts, stated as of a piece with each other.

**2a.** Co-evolution as described in the core requirement: species descended from a common ancestor,
co-evolving with each other **and** with the local environment — which the life forms both *shape* and
*are shaped by*, and which **in part encodes history**.

**2b.** Stated verbatim by Greg (written, in his words, while high — and he asks that it be deciphered
rather than discarded, noting it is of a piece with the evolution goal above):

> "to demonstrate invasive vs native behavior via entrenched ecosystems that encourage the co-evolution
> of species of plants suited to their idiosyncratic environments / local conditions, and these
> populations are mostly kept isolated but come from a common ancestor, and then suddenly after eons and
> eons of secluded co-evolution between each other and the environment which records in some sense or
> encodes the particulars of local history there, you suddenly lower the gates vastly and let species
> intermingle and become invasive and drastically change the balance of the ecosystem but at the same
> time still adapt and find homeostasis after sufficient eons of time."

**Clarified restatement of 2b** (a reading of the above, no content added):

1. Start from a single common ancestor.
2. Distribute descendant populations into pockets that are **mostly isolated** from one another.
3. Each pocket has **idiosyncratic local conditions**.
4. Over **eons**, each pocket's species co-evolve with each other and with their local environment.
5. The local environment comes to **record or encode the particulars of that pocket's history**.
6. Then **the gates are lowered, vastly and suddenly**, and populations intermingle.
7. Some species behave as **invasives** and drastically change the balance of the ecosystems.
8. Given **sufficient further time**, the system still adapts and finds a **new homeostasis**.

### Goal 3 — Emergent biophilic form

- Encourage the emergence of biophilic forms **without hard-coding them**: functional leaves, seeds,
  flowers, branching, beauty.
- On buds-as-agents that leave behind a trail of structural/transport cells: this has been explored
  before. He is **not** saying avoid it — he thinks it is probably worthwhile and there is a reason he
  keeps gravitating to it. The point is only to be sure the new work does not **reinvent what already
  failed**.

---

## 3. Constraints and Explicit Non-Goals

- **Do not hard-code predator/prey relationships.**
- **Do not hard-code trophic stratification** of the ecosystem — it should emerge by itself given
  enough time and space.
- Homeostasis should **emerge naturally**, not be hard-coded (à la Lotka–Volterra in behavior, but
  arrived at rather than imposed).
- Ecosystems should tend toward homeostasis but **always outrun it** — "like a planet falling into the
  sun but actually just orbiting it."
- Keep rules simple and legible while still producing a near-infinite possibility space.

---

## 4. Open Questions Greg Raised

1. **Has this already been accomplished anywhere?** If not, what building blocks could get there, with
   a breakthrough or two?
2. **How many basic plant shapes / behaviors / modules are there actually?** He lists as examples: leaf,
   branch, extend, thicken, widen/narrow, flower type. His intuition: you could always split into
   subdivisions, but at low resolution there seems to be a discrete number of essential
   elements/moves.
3. **How many independent-yet-interacting elements are needed to comprise an environment** capable of
   being shaped by and shaping life forms — especially in ways that can be simply, elegantly, legibly
   laid out? His candidate list:
   - water / moisture
   - light / energy
   - earth / soil / sand
   - air / emptiness / space
   - time, both linear and cyclical
   - heat / coolness
   - attraction / repulsion
   - pressure
   - velocity and acceleration

## 5. Unstructured Thoughts (his, offered as potentially fertile — fine to cull or ignore)

- **The card-shuffle intuition.** A perfectly shuffled deck yields more permutations than there are
  atoms in the universe. So it should be possible to keep things fairly simple and legible while still
  producing an immense, near-infinite possibility space — enough for accidental evolution to feed back
  into itself and create a billion different variations, paths, approaches, and strategies for survival.
- **Countless legible unique microbiomes.** It should be quite possible to create countless legible,
  unique microbiomes that give rise to their own particular life forms, shaped over eons to perfectly
  and complexly complement the environment and the other life forms which partly comprise and/or
  influence it.
