What am I watching?

Bottom: a heated red chamber. Middle: red crust, then blue ocean. Above: empty black space. Cutaway hides the front of the same volume so you can look inside. It does not remove simulated matter. Tap “Whole” to see all of it.

Heat enters at the floor. Hot earth exchanges places with cooler earth, guided by a coarse fluid field. A simplified chamber stores pressure from heating. When that exceeds the weakest roof’s strength, a connected column moves upward: magma leaves its chamber, displacing real crust and water. Discharge lowers pressure. Lava transfers heat to water and freezes into rock; sufficiently hot water becomes vapor, then cools and falls again.

Every occupied cube is one persistent parcel. Red stays earth, blue stays water. The brighter melt is hotter; dull red grains are loose earth. Blue vapor is fainter than liquid. Purple can appear only where blue water is seen in front of red earth. The camera changes nothing in the physics.

Earth —Water —

Honest limits. This is a stylized lattice experiment, not a calibrated geology model or full multiphase Navier–Stokes simulation. The pressure law is a reduced-order approximation; the flow solver only guides local exchanges. Heat is accounted for against an external heater and cooling boundary, but pressure work and gravitational energy are not included. Solid islands and water-driven grain transport are experimental; rivers, glaciers and the poster’s full geography are not implemented. A finite closed world may eventually settle.

Reset creates a new initial world. It is the only control that changes the matter inventory’s history.
