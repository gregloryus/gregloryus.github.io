Rules, controls & MVP limits
Green: living plant. Gold: mobile seed. Ochre: sand. White: petrified plant. Tap to inspect a cell; Add seeds is an explicit intervention, never automatic reseeding.


Each genome is a depth-first sequence of left / forward / right triples. Relative orientation is inherited through branches. Mutation adds or removes a terminal node, up to 10 nodes. Plants collect energy through exposed stem cells, pay for growth, and release mutated offspring from their final genome tip. Offspring compete for space. Plants live 720 ticks.


Seeds fall and germinate only with solid support directly below (sand, living plant, fossil, or the bottom boundary). At death, the first occurrence of an exact genome petrifies in place; later occurrences become ordinary falling sand. White fossils never fall except when a full bottom row shifts the world down. Sand has no nutrients or decay. Crevices are allowed to jam the world. Only the bottom row clears; upper full rows remain. The implicit floor lies below the visible grid, so it is never itself cleared.
This is a shader-native reinterpretation, not exact Simplant v20: finite genomes, synchronous growth arbitration, direct root energy credit instead of traveling light packets, fixed lifespan instead of starvation, no distance bonus. The grid is fixed at 256 × 192. Growth, exposure, energy, mutation, reproduction, movement and rendering use integer GPU textures. CPU work schedules passes, handles controls and reads occasional statistics. Every 32 ticks, a CPU registry checks dying roots using exact packed genomes, chooses one first occurrence, and sends verdicts to the GPU. GPU passes perform the actual petrification, sand conversion and row shift. This occasional readback is a performance cost; this fork is deliberately hybrid for global novelty bookkeeping. The registry lasts until Reset/reload and can be exported.


Seed 14 and the same interventions at the same ticks reproduce the same run. Pausing a hidden tab preserves ticks. This MVP is not a demonstration of open-ended evolution: the genome space is bounded. No network access or dependencies.
