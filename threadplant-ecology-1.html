How this prototype works

Bright tips are the only developmental agents. They carry a 64-direction heading and move one lattice cell at a time, leaving conduit behind. Touching cells of the same genet form the transport graph; there are no parent pointers or stored branches.

The world begins without soil: only a pool of water under open space. Dead tissue and failed seeds become falling organic matter, so plants manufacture the substrate later generations encounter. Deep organic matter slowly leaves through the floor, making the world's memory persistent but not permanent.

Blue packets enter through root-polarized tissue touching water. With the water cycle on, blue traffic enables exposed shoot tissue to make red energy and sometimes transpire water into vapor. Both colors perform ordinary random walks through adjacent tissue of the same genet only. A tip briefly latches one of each; red is spent on an action and blue returns to the network.

Every tip has a lineage ID, root/shoot polarity, fine-grained heading, and a current place within its seed's developmental grammar. A genome contains a variable number of modules. Each module encodes length and curvature, repetition, branching, module calls, environmental plasticity, and reproductive tendency.

The grammar supplies a heritable morphological theme while openness or wetness can bend its execution locally. Mutations can tune a module, rewire module calls, or duplicate and delete whole modules. Thus genome complexity and recursive body structure can expand rather than choosing among a fixed table of local reactions.

Directional light makes height and self-shading matter. A reproductive instruction grows a persistent golden bud that must receive repeated red-blue pairs before releasing a seed. Traffic maintains aging tissue but cannot make it immortal.

Support is also local: contact with ground propagates through touching living or woody cells, even across lineages. Unsupported tissue loses vitality and breaks down. Thus plants can incidentally prop up or depend upon neighbors without sharing resources.
