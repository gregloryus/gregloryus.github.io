What am I watching?

Bottom: a heated red chamber. Middle: red crust, then blue ocean. Above: empty black space. Cutaway hides the front of the same volume so you can look inside. It does not remove simulated matter. Tap “Whole” to see all of it.

Heat enters at the floor. Hot earth exchanges places with cooler earth, guided by a coarse fluid field. A simplified chamber stores pressure from basal heating. When that exceeds the weakest roof’s strength, magma displaces existing crust and water. Discharge lowers pressure. Lava cools into rock. Every surface earth parcel gets a gravity check; rock needs a connected support path to the buried boundary or floor. Detached material falls and settles.

Water now completes a cycle. Sunlight heats exposed water into rising vapor. Wind carries it; the cooler upper air condenses it into falling blue rain. Water pools and runs down the terrain. Moving water gradually darkens and loosens rock, rolls the red grains downhill, and deposits them where flow subsides. All rain and sediment are existing parcels, tracked through each change.

Every occupied cube is one persistent parcel. Red stays earth, blue stays water. The brighter melt is hotter; dull red grains are loose earth. Blue vapor is fainter than liquid. Purple can appear only where blue water is seen in front of red earth. The camera changes nothing in the physics.

Earth —Water —

Honest limits. This is a stylized lattice experiment, not a calibrated geology model or full multiphase Navier–Stokes simulation. Shallow starting water makes land appear sooner. Pressure, phase transitions, wind and upper-air cooling use simplified rules. Heat is accounted for against basal heating, sunlight and cooling; pressure work and gravitational energy are not included. Runoff and sediment transport are simulated, but a developed river network, glaciers and the poster’s full geography are not guaranteed. A finite closed world may eventually settle.

Reset creates a new initial world. It is the only control that changes the matter inventory’s history.
